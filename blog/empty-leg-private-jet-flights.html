Empty Leg Private Jet Flights Explained

An empty leg private jet flight can be one of the best-value ways to experience private travel, provided your plans are flexible. Here is a clear explanation of what they are, why they cost less, and how to catch one.

Empty legs are among the most talked-about ideas in private aviation, and often the most misunderstood. Used well, they offer access to a private cabin at a fraction of the usual outlay. Used carelessly, they can lead to disappointment. This guide sets out exactly how they work, so you can judge whether an empty leg is right for your next trip by private jet charter.

What is an empty leg?

Private aircraft rarely stay in one place. When a jet flies a client from London to Nice, for example, it frequently has to fly back to its home base, or on to its next assignment, with no passengers aboard. That repositioning flight is the empty leg. The aircraft is going to make the journey regardless, so rather than fly it empty, the operator offers those seats, or the whole cabin, at a reduced rate to anyone whose plans happen to align.

In short, an empty leg is a flight that already exists. You are not commissioning a bespoke journey; you are stepping into one that the aircraft's schedule has created.

Why are empty legs cheaper?

The economics are straightforward. The cost of the repositioning flight is, in effect, already accounted for. Any revenue the operator can recover from that otherwise-empty leg is a bonus, which is why these flights are typically offered well below the equivalent point-to-point charter price. It is a genuine win-win: the traveller enjoys a substantial saving, and the operator offsets a cost it would otherwise absorb.

That said, prices remain indicative and depend on the aircraft, the route and how far in advance the opportunity arises. For a fuller picture of what standard charter involves, see our guide on how much it costs to charter a private jet.

The trade-offs

Empty legs are not a free lunch, and it pays to understand the compromises before you set your heart on one:

  • Fixed routes: The origin and destination are set by the original charter. You take the journey the aircraft is already making, not one designed around you.
  • Fixed dates and times: Empty legs happen when they happen. There is limited scope to move a departure by even a few hours.
  • Short notice: Many empty legs surface only days, sometimes hours, before departure, which rewards travellers who can move quickly.
  • Possible changes: Because an empty leg depends on the original charter, it can be amended or cancelled if that booking changes. Flexibility on both sides is essential.

For all these reasons, empty legs suit the flexible traveller far better than anyone tied to a precise itinerary. If your dates and destination are non-negotiable, a conventional charter, chosen with help from our guide on how to choose the right private jet size, is usually the wiser route.

Who are empty legs best for?

The ideal candidate is someone with a destination in mind but room to manoeuvre on timing, or someone happy to be led by opportunity. Leisure travellers heading to popular hotspots such as Ibiza or the French Riviera often find well-timed empty legs during the busy season, when aircraft are repositioning frequently between the same city pairs. If you can keep your plans loose, the value can be exceptional.

Frequently asked questions

What is an empty leg private jet flight?+

An empty leg is a repositioning flight an aircraft must make with no passengers, for example returning to base after dropping guests off. Because the aircraft is flying anyway, that leg can be offered at a substantially reduced rate to a traveller whose plans happen to match.

How much can you save on an empty leg?+

Savings vary widely with the aircraft and route, but empty legs are typically offered well below the equivalent point-to-point charter price. All figures are indicative and confirmed only on a firm quote, as availability changes constantly.

What are the drawbacks of empty legs?+

Empty legs are fixed in route, date and often time, and they can be cancelled or rescheduled if the original charter that created them changes. They suit flexible travellers who can adapt to what is available rather than dictate their own schedule.
