Light, Midsize or Heavy Jet? How to Choose the Right Private Jet

Knowing how to choose a private jet comes down to three questions: how many are travelling, how far, and what matters most on board. This guide matches each aircraft category to passengers, range and budget so you can charter with confidence.

Private jets are grouped into broad categories by cabin size and range. The right category is the one that carries your party the distance in comfort without paying for capacity you will not use. Get it right and the aircraft feels tailored to the trip; get it wrong and you are either cramped or over-specified. Below we walk through each category, then bring them together in a comparison table.

Light jets

Light jets typically seat four to seven passengers and are the workhorses of European charter. They offer excellent value on short sectors such as London to Paris, Zurich or Edinburgh, with enough baggage space for a weekend or a business trip. The cabin usually has standing headroom towards the centre and a modest galley. If your journey is under a couple of hours with a small party, a light jet is often the sweet spot.

Midsize and super-midsize jets

Midsize jets step up to a taller, wider cabin, seating around seven to nine, with a fully enclosed lavatory and more baggage volume, ideal for Mediterranean runs to Nice, Ibiza or Palma. Super-midsize aircraft add still more range and cabin space, comfortably flying longer European and near-transatlantic sectors while keeping running costs below those of a heavy jet. For most leisure groups and business teams, this is the versatile middle ground.

Heavy and ultra-long-range jets

Heavy jets offer a spacious stand-up cabin, a flat bed and the range for intercontinental travel. At the top sit ultra-long-range aircraft such as the Gulfstream G650 and Bombardier Global 7500, which fly non-stop to New York, Dubai and beyond with a full cabin. Matching aircraft to your journey

Start with distance, then party size, then comfort. A four-person day trip to Geneva does not need a heavy jet; a fourteen-strong group heading to New York certainly does. Baggage matters too, as ski gear, golf clubs and film equipment all eat into cabin space. Frequently asked questions

What size private jet do I need for a European trip?+

For most European trips a light or midsize jet is ideal, carrying six to nine passengers with room for luggage. Longer Mediterranean routes suit a super-midsize cabin.

Which private jet is best for transatlantic flights?+

Transatlantic and Gulf routes call for a heavy or ultra-long-range jet, which can fly non-stop with a full cabin and offer a flat bed and stand-up headroom.

Does a bigger jet always cost more?+

Generally yes, as larger aircraft carry higher hourly rates. The right choice balances passengers, range and comfort against budget, which is where our advice helps.
