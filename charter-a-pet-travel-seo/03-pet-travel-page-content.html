Why owners charter privately to fly with a pet

Most airlines flying to and from the UK will not carry a pet in the cabin at any price. Animals travel as manifest cargo: crated, checked in hours early at a freight facility, flown in the hold, and collected from a cargo shed at the other end. For an elderly dog, an anxious cat or a snub-nosed breed that many airlines refuse outright, that is a hard journey — and for many owners, simply not an option.

A private charter removes all of it. Your pet boards with you across the apron, settles in the cabin with you, and walks off with you. No crate in the hold, no separation, no queues — and on longer routes, water, comfort breaks and a crew who genuinely like having a dog on board.

Flying pets into the UK — our speciality

Bringing a pet into Great Britain is where private charter earns its keep. Arrivals with animals must land at an airport approved to receive them, where the required checks are completed before your pet is cleared — and unlike commercial cargo handling, it happens quickly and with you present. We arrange pet arrivals at approved airports including London Biggin Hill — where the dedicated SkyPets reception team meets our flights — as well as Farnborough, London Luton, Oxford and Manchester, and we'll confirm the best arrival airport for your itinerary.

In brief, a dog, cat or ferret entering Great Britain needs:

  • a microchip, fitted before the rabies vaccination;
  • a valid rabies vaccination, given at least 21 days before travel;
  • the right document for the route — an EU pet passport, an animal health certificate (AHC) issued in GB within the last four months for returning pets, or a GB pet health certificate when arriving from further afield;
  • for dogs, a vet-administered tapeworm treatment 24 hours to 5 days before arrival in GB.

Rules change — the UK and EU are phasing in new arrangements — so treat this as a guide: our team verifies the current requirements for every booking. For the full walkthrough, timeline and checklist, read our guide to flying pets into the UK by private jet.

Big dogs, several pets, unusual companions

Private charter is flexible where airlines are rigid. Large breeds travel loose in the cabin on a suitable aircraft; multiple dogs, a dog-and-cat household, even birds can be accommodated, subject to the operator's approval and destination rules. When several animals travel together, or a journey counts as a commercial movement, extra paperwork applies — flag it early and we'll build it into the plan.

Frequently asked questions

Can my dog sit with me in the cabin?

Yes — that's the point. On a Charter-A pet friendly private jet charter your dog travels in the cabin with you, never in the hold. Most airlines flying into the UK only carry pets as cargo, which is why owners charter privately.

Typically a microchip, a rabies vaccination at least 21 days old, and an EU pet passport or animal health certificate; dogs entering GB also need a tapeworm treatment 24 hours to 5 days before arrival. Requirements vary by route — we confirm exactly what applies when you book.

Pets arriving in Great Britain by private charter must land at an approved airport. We regularly use London Biggin Hill (with its SkyPets reception team), Farnborough, Luton, Oxford and Manchester, and will confirm the right arrival point for your trip.

Far fewer than on airlines. Snub-nosed breeds that airlines refuse, large dogs and multiple pets can usually be accommodated, subject to operator approval and UK law on restricted breeds.
