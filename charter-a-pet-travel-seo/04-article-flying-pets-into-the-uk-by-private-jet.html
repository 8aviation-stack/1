If you have ever tried to bring a dog or cat into the United Kingdom, you'll know the uncomfortable truth: airlines flying into the UK will not carry your pet in the cabin. Whatever their policy elsewhere in the world, animals arriving in Britain on a scheduled flight travel as manifest cargo — crated, in the hold, checked in at a freight facility and collected from one. For most owners, a pet friendly private jet charter is the only way a pet actually enters the UK sitting beside them.

The good news is that flying a pet into the UK privately is a well-trodden path — Charter-A arranges it year-round — and the rules, while strict, are entirely manageable when the timeline is planned properly. Here is how it works in practice.

Why private charter is the in-cabin route into Britain

The UK's pet travel rules don't just govern paperwork — they govern how and where an animal may arrive. On scheduled airlines the approved method into the UK is cargo carriage, which is why even cabin-approved pets flying out of the UK must come home in the hold. Private charter operates under a different arrangement: the aircraft lands at an airport approved to receive pets, an authorised checker verifies your animal's documents and microchip — with you standing there — and your pet clears in minutes rather than hours in a cargo shed.

What your pet needs to enter Great Britain

For dogs, cats and ferrets, the core requirements are:

  • Microchip — fitted and readable before the rabies vaccination, or the vaccination doesn't count.
  • Rabies vaccination — your pet must be at least 12 weeks old when vaccinated, and you must wait 21 full days after the primary vaccination before travel.
  • The right document for your route:
    • an EU pet passport (issued in the EU, or in GB before 1 January 2021);
    • an animal health certificate (AHC) issued in GB — used by UK-based pets returning home, valid for re-entry for four months from issue; or
    • a GB pet health certificate, for pets arriving from countries outside the EU passport system, such as the USA.
  • Tapeworm treatment (dogs only) — a vet must administer a praziquantel-based treatment no less than 24 hours and no more than 5 days (120 hours) before arrival in GB, recorded in the passport or certificate. Time zones catch people out on transatlantic trips — we sanity-check the window against your actual landing time.

Get these right and there is no quarantine. Get them wrong and the consequences are serious — quarantine or refusal of entry at the owner's expense — which is exactly why our operations team re-verifies every document before departure. Rules also evolve: a new UK–EU agreement is expected to simplify pet documents in the coming period, so always confirm current requirements (the official source is gov.uk/bring-pet-to-great-britain) — or let us do it for you.

Frequently asked questions

Can pets really fly in the cabin into the UK?

Yes — by private charter. Scheduled airlines carry pets into the UK as cargo only; on a private jet your pet travels in the cabin with you and is checked in your presence at an approved arrival airport.

Is there quarantine when flying a pet into the UK?

No — provided the microchip, rabies vaccination, documentation and (for dogs) the tapeworm treatment window are all correct. Non-compliance can mean quarantine, which is why the paperwork is checked before the aircraft ever departs.

How long before the flight should I start preparing?

If your pet is already microchipped and rabies-vaccinated, two to three weeks is comfortable. Starting from scratch, allow at least a month — the 21-day post-vaccination wait sets the floor.

Can I fly my dog from the USA to the UK in the cabin?

Only by private charter. With a GB pet health certificate, correctly timed tapeworm treatment and an approved arrival airport, your dog flies non-stop in the cabin — no cargo facility at either end.
